A free set of writing rules for Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, and more

Make AI writing sound precise, direct, and natural.

Clean Language removes the habits that make AI writing irritating: robotic tone, filler, fake enthusiasm, and repeated conclusions. It also cuts contrast framing, business jargon, excessive bullets, unnecessary em and en dashes, and predictable vocabulary such as delve, unlock, and transformative.

Clean Language is a small file of written rules you add to your AI once. It tells your AI to keep your facts, figures, names, quotations, and technical terms exactly as you wrote them; when a style rule and the meaning conflict, the meaning wins.

What Clean Language fixes

The writing patterns people notice.

Robotic AI tone

Removes polished but empty prose, mechanical transitions, repetitive sentence shapes, and language that sounds generated rather than written.

Contrast framing

Rewrites formulas such as “it is not X, it is Y,” “not because X, but because Y,” and “this is not merely X.”

Em dash and en dash overuse

Uses commas, colons, parentheses, or complete sentences where they communicate the idea more cleanly.

AI filler words

Challenges phrases such as “it is important to note,” “at its core,” “in today’s landscape,” “delve,” “tapestry,” “leverage,” and “unlock.”

Fake enthusiasm

Removes automatic praise, reassurance, validation, inspirational framing, and unsupported claims that something is critical or transformative.

Over-explaining

Cuts repeated conclusions, unnecessary summaries, obvious implications, long introductions, and paragraphs that announce what the next paragraph will say.

Bullet list abuse

Collapses the bullet staircase, where every sentence becomes a bullet without adding structure, and keeps connected reasoning in paragraphs.

Hidden ownership

Names the person, team, system, control, consequence, or required action when vague language would conceal responsibility.

Artificial balance

Stops forcing a positive counterpoint after every criticism. Findings remain clear unless mitigating evidence changes the conclusion.

Designed for real work

Writing that stays precise.

Clean Language focuses on meaning, clarity, accountability, and natural cadence. It improves the writing itself; it does not try to fool AI detectors.

It supports email, Teams, Slack, LinkedIn, reports, executive communication, technical documentation, governance material, policies, incident updates, and general prose.

Questions

What Clean Language is for.

Is Clean Language an AI humanizer?

Clean Language makes generated text more natural by improving the writing itself. It instructs the AI to preserve correctness and does not target AI-detector scores.

Does it ban every em dash, en dash, adverb, or passive sentence?

No. Clean Language keeps punctuation and constructions that carry precise meaning, including dashes, and removes only the decorative ones. You can instruct it to prohibit dashes entirely when that is your preference.

Can it clean writing produced by Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or another model?

Yes. The rules work with any text-generating AI that accepts persistent instructions or supplied reference text. Use them as an installed skill, paste them as instructions, or follow them yourself as a checklist.

Is it suitable for technical and policy writing?

Yes. The precedence rules protect commands, identifiers, legal language, obligations, standards terminology, factual qualifications, and domain-specific vocabulary.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free for personal and commercial use, under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International licence.

What exactly am I adding to my AI?

A small skill file of text instructions, configuration, and icons that your AI reads. It is not a program, nothing is installed on your computer, and it does not connect to anything. You can switch it off or remove it at any time, from the same place you added it; removing it changes nothing else in your account.

Why did Jeff Posluns create Clean Language?

Jeff Posluns, an IT and cybersecurity executive, created Clean Language after one too many AI-generated documents arrived copy-pasted and unread by their own senders. He wrote the first version in about an hour, drawing on his GRC Library (opens in a new tab) project (source on GitHub (opens in a new tab)), and it has been revised steadily since. It exists to make everyone's AI output a little less infuriating to read.

Can I use American spelling?

Clean Language uses Oxford English spelling with -ize endings by default: colour, centre, organize. If you prefer American spelling, add this line to your instructions: Use American spelling: color, center, organize. See the install guide.
