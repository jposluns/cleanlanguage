How this site handles your data

Privacy.

This site has no accounts, no sign-in, and no forms, so you never hand it anything to store. We do not track you across the web, build a profile of you, or sell data about you, and we run no analytics of our own. Other companies deliver the site, the downloads, and our email, and their systems see your requests and keep their own records.

Who is responsible for this site

Clean Language is published by Jeff Posluns, in Canada, who is responsible for any personal data this site involves. You can reach the privacy contact at privacy@cleanlanguage.ai. We do not publish a postal address, because the site collects nothing directly and email is the faster route to a reply. Jeff Posluns is also the appointed data protection officer, reachable at the same address.

What reaches us when you visit

You fill in nothing and upload nothing by reading the site, because there is no form here. Your browser still sends technical data with every request, as it does to any website.

What a web request carries is technical data, which for this site reaches Cloudflare rather than us. It may include your IP address, the address and query you requested, the time, your browser's user-agent and referrer headers, and routing and security signals. From those, Cloudflare can derive attributes such as country, browser, operating system, and device type. It uses them to deliver the pages, to keep the site secure, and to produce traffic analytics.

Those analytics are the only measurement available to us. They are counts and derived attributes, including a unique-visitor figure that Cloudflare calculates from IP addresses, so they are not purely anonymous. We do not use them to identify you, and we do not combine them with anything else. You do not have to create an account or accept tracking to read anything here.

Cookies and storage

The site's own code sets no cookies and includes no client-side analytics. It uses no local storage or session storage in your browser. The site's 2 small scripts, which handle the copy buttons and the install picker, make no network calls and write nothing to your browser's storage; the copy button writes the text you are looking at to your clipboard, and only when you click it. You can read both scripts in the published source.

Cloudflare, which serves the site, processes request data such as IP addresses for delivery, security, and aggregate traffic analytics, and may set a security cookie. That cookie helps distinguish human visitors from automated traffic. It carries no advertising function.

Who else is involved

  • Cloudflare hosts the site, serves the Markdown instruction files, and carries the privacy mailbox described below, under its own privacy policy (opens in a new tab). It also runs the email routing described below.
  • GitHub serves the skill archive and the source code. Following a download or source link makes a request to GitHub and the content delivery servers it redirects downloads to, which keep their own records under its own privacy statement (opens in a new tab). If you report a problem by opening a GitHub issue, your GitHub account name and everything you write there are normally public, and GitHub notifies the maintainer.
  • The sites we link to. The clickable links on these pages go to external sites including GitHub, GitHub Docs, LinkedIn, Creative Commons, Cloudflare, and grclibrary.ai. Following any link tells that site you visited it, under that site's own terms.

We do not sell personal data, disclose it for advertising, or pass it to data brokers. Data does reach the companies above, because delivering a website and an email address requires it, and it reaches any site you choose to click through to. Beyond that we would disclose data only where the law required it. Most of the data that could be demanded sits with those companies rather than with us. The exception is the correspondence described below, which we do hold.

If you write to us

Writing to the privacy address is the one place where you choose to give us personal data. Your message passes through Cloudflare Email Routing, which records routing events such as sender, recipient, subject, and delivery outcome, and delivers it to a Cloudflare Worker behind the privacy and data protection interface that Jeff Posluns runs across his projects. It does not go out to a separate email provider. We use what you send only to answer you, and we add you to no list. Messages stay in that interface until Jeff Posluns deletes them, so there is no fixed retention period.

A message can contain whatever its sender chooses to put in it, including sensitive details we never asked for. Please send only what your question needs.

Where data is processed

Cloudflare and GitHub operate globally, so your request data may be processed on servers outside Canada, including in the United States. We hold no copy of that data ourselves. Each provider publishes its own terms and safeguards for the service it runs, linked above, and we have not put separate transfer agreements in place beyond the terms those services are supplied under.

How long data is kept

The site itself keeps nothing, because it stores nothing. Cloudflare and GitHub retain their own logs and analytics for their own periods, set by them under their own policies, and Cloudflare retains email routing events for a period it sets. We do not set, extend, or control any of those periods. The only records we hold are the messages people send to the privacy address. They sit in the privacy interface described above until Jeff Posluns deletes them.

The Clean Language skill itself

The Clean Language skill is a small set of Markdown files with an icon and a configuration file. It contains no executable code and makes no network calls, and it runs inside your own AI assistant. It carries no telemetry and sends us no prompt and no response, so we never see your writing through it. What the AI provider you installed it in does with your text is governed by that provider's own terms, which we neither control nor describe here. The only writing that reaches us is what you choose to send: an email to us, or a post in a GitHub issue.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, data protection law may give you rights over personal data about you, including the rights to see it, to correct it, to have it erased, to restrict or object to how it is used, and to complain to a supervisory authority. Where any processing rests on your consent, you can withdraw it.

Send any request about this site to privacy@cleanlanguage.ai. We can act directly on the correspondence we hold, and we will. We do not control the logs and analytics that Cloudflare and GitHub keep, and we can neither search nor delete them, so for those we will tell you what we know and what we cannot do rather than promise a result you would not get. Where you have dealt with GitHub directly, by downloading a release or opening an issue, GitHub answers for its own platform records. Issue content also reaches us as the repository owner, and we answer for what we do with it. We will reply as promptly as we can, and within any period the law that applies to you requires. If you are in Canada, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. Elsewhere, where the law that applies to you gives you that right, you can complain to the competent privacy or data protection authority.

Other privacy points

  • Data from other sources. We buy no data about you and append nothing from data brokers. What we receive from others is the traffic analytics Cloudflare derives from requests, and GitHub's notifications about issues.
  • Automated decisions about you. We make no automated decisions that have a legal or similarly significant effect on you, and we do not profile visitors. Cloudflare's automated security filtering separates human from automated traffic as part of delivering the site, and makes no such decision about you.
  • Children's data. The site is not directed at children, asks nobody for personal data, and operates no age check. A child who visits generates the same request data as any other visitor, and anyone who writes to us sends whatever their message contains.
