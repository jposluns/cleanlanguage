How this site handles your data

Privacy.

This site does not try to find out who you are. It has no accounts, no sign-in, and no forms, so there is nothing for you to fill in and nothing for us to store. We do not track you across the web, build a profile of you, or sell or share data about you. One qualification is honest and unavoidable. Like every website, this one is delivered by other companies whose systems necessarily see your requests, and those companies keep their own records. This page explains exactly what that means.

Who is responsible for this site

Clean Language is published by Jeff Posluns, in Canada, who is responsible for any personal data this site involves. You can reach the privacy contact at privacy@cleanlanguage.ai. We do not publish a postal address, because the site collects no personal data directly and email is the faster route to a reply. No data protection officer is appointed.

What reaches us when you visit

You do not send us anything by reading the site. There is no field to complete and no file to upload.

What any website receives is the technical data your own browser sends with each request: your IP address, the page requested, your browser type, and an approximate location that the network derives from the IP address. Our host processes that data to deliver the pages, to keep the site secure, and to produce aggregate traffic counts. Those counts are the only measurement we have, and they describe pages rather than people. We do not use this data to identify you and we do not combine it with anything else. You do not have to create an account or accept tracking to read anything here. We collect no special categories of data, such as data about your health, beliefs, or background.

Cookies and storage

The site's own code sets no cookies and includes no client-side analytics. It uses no local storage or session storage in your browser. The site's two small scripts, which handle the copy buttons and the install picker, store nothing and make no network calls, and you can read them in the published source.

Cloudflare, which serves the site, processes request data such as IP addresses for delivery, security, and aggregate traffic analytics, and may set a security cookie. That cookie helps distinguish human visitors from automated traffic. It carries no advertising function.

Who else is involved

Three other parties can receive data through your use of this site.

  • Cloudflare hosts the site and handles your request data as described above, under its own privacy policy (opens in a new tab).
  • GitHub serves the downloadable skill and the source code. Following a download or source link makes a request to GitHub, which keeps its own records under its own privacy statement (opens in a new tab).
  • The sites we link to. Links from these pages go to GitHub, LinkedIn, Creative Commons, posluns.ca, and grclibrary.ai. Following any link tells that site you visited it, under that site's own terms.

We do not sell or share personal data. We would disclose data only where the law required it, and in practice the data that could be demanded is held by Cloudflare or GitHub rather than by us.

If you write to us

Writing to the privacy address is the one place where you choose to give us personal data. Your message reaches us through Cloudflare Email Routing, which forwards it to the maintainer's mailbox. We use what you send only to answer you, we do not add you to any list, and we keep the correspondence no longer than we need to deal with your question and any follow-up to it.

Where data is processed

Cloudflare and GitHub are global providers, so your request data may be processed on servers outside Canada, including in the United States. We hold no copy of that data ourselves, and we rely on the safeguards each provider publishes for its own service rather than on separate transfer agreements. Each provider's current terms are on its own site, linked above.

How long data is kept

The site keeps nothing, because it stores nothing. Cloudflare and GitHub retain their own request logs for their own periods, set by them under their own policies. We do not set, extend, or control those periods, and we hold no copy of that data to retain.

The Clean Language skill itself

The Clean Language skill is a text file that runs inside your own AI assistant. It sends nothing to us, and we never receive your writing. When you use it, your text stays between you and whichever AI you installed it in, and that provider's own terms govern what happens to it.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, data protection law may give you rights over personal data about you, including the rights to see it, to correct it, to have it erased, to restrict or object to how it is used, and to complain to a supervisory authority. Where any processing rests on your consent, you can withdraw it. This site does not rely on your consent to operate, because it asks you for nothing.

Because we hold no accounts and store no personal data, there is little for us to act on directly. The request data described above sits with Cloudflare or GitHub, who can act on it. For anything we do control, write to privacy@cleanlanguage.ai and we will reply as promptly as we can, and within any period the law that applies to you requires. If you are in Canada, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada. If you are elsewhere, you can complain to your local data protection authority.

What this page does not cover

Three standard privacy topics do not apply here. We state them rather than leave them unanswered.

  • Data from other sources. We obtain no data about you from anyone else. Everything described above comes directly from your own browser's request, or from a message you chose to send us.
  • Automated decisions about you. We make no automated decisions that have a legal or similarly significant effect on you, and we do not profile visitors. Cloudflare's automated security filtering separates human from automated traffic as part of delivering the site, and it makes no such decision about you.
  • Children's data. The site is not directed at children and knowingly collects no personal data from anyone, so it operates no age check and no parental consent process. A child who visits generates the same request data as any other visitor.

Changes to this page
